# ARHN — Choi et al. (arXiv 2604.11092v1, April 2026), "Answer-Centric Relabeling of Hard Negatives with Open-Source LLMs for Dense Retrieval"

Re-read 2026-11-14 from the stored PDF: body and appendices read page by page; the reference list (pp. 9 to 10) was checked for the entries the text cites rather than read entry by entry.

Source file: arXiv PDF v1, 10 pages (body pp.1-8, references pp.8-10). Text retrieval, not code. Included as the most recent worked example of a rank-based, positive-anchored rule for editing a training example's negatives and positives.

## What it established

- A two-stage open-model pipeline edits each training triplet (one positive, up to 10 mined hard negatives): stage 1 extracts a verbatim answer span from every document or returns NO_ANSWER; stage 2 ranks the spans in a list. A negative ranked above the positive is promoted to positive; a negative ranked below the positive but with an answer span is dropped from the negatives; a negative with no span is kept (Sec. 3, pp.3-4).
- Fine-tuning a 110M encoder on the pruned 680K-pair BGE subset, the combined edit (relabel plus filter) gives nDCG@10 0.521 on 16 BEIR datasets against 0.508 without refinement, 0.511 for percentage-of-positive-score negative selection and 0.515 for the closed-model relabelling baseline (Table 1, p.6). Relabelling alone (0.511) and filtering alone (0.510) are each close to the baseline (Sec. 5.1.3, p.7).
- Labeller size matters in sign: the 32B labeller helps, the 8B labeller lowers the average from 0.508 to 0.501 (Sec. 5.3, p.8, Figure 4); smaller labellers relabel and filter more (3.1 and 3.9 negatives per query for 8B against 1.6 and 2.2 for 32B, Table 4, p.7). Agreement with two human assessors rises with size (Cohen's kappa 0.312, 0.341, 0.373; Table 3, p.7).
- Listwise ranking over extracted spans beats listwise ranking over whole passages (0.521 against 0.516, Table 2, p.7).

## What it did not establish

- Nothing about code, repositories or issue queries; the "answer" notion has no direct equivalent for a function that must be edited.
- The training-set selection (seven datasets) is inherited from a paper that chose it on BEIR scores, so absolute BEIR numbers are not a clean held-out figure. Seeds: "multiple random seeds" with the mean reported; no intervals, no per-dataset significance (Sec. 4.3, p.5).
- Gains of 0.8 to 1.3 points on a 16-dataset average sit beside per-dataset differences of both signs (Table 1, p.6); no test is reported.
- Relabelling and filtering are not compared at a matched number of updates or of positives; they change the number of positives per query.

## Method details we need

- Rule (Sec. 3.2.2, p.4): with the original positive as anchor, promote a negative whose span ranks above the positive's (Eq. 8), drop a negative ranked below the positive that still has a span (Eq. 9), keep span-less negatives (Eq. 10). The decision is relative to the positive, not an absolute threshold.
- Training (Sec. 4.3, p.5): InfoNCE, one positive, seven hard negatives plus in-batch negatives, batch 128; 110M encoder five epochs at 2e-5; 7B-based encoder with LoRA, two epochs at 1e-4.
- Labeller: Qwen3-32B for the reported rows; 10 negatives per query; 16 A100 GPUs for the labelling pass (Sec. 4.1, p.5).

## Relation to RQ1 / RQ2 / RQ3

- RQ1, RQ3: none.
- RQ2: a rank-relative-to-the-positive edit is the same kind of object as a K-rank cut (the positive's own position decides the action). It supports "cleaning a triplet beats weighting alone" only for text, and its own table shows the relabel-only and filter-only arms near the baseline. It also shows an editing signal can hurt when its source is weak (8B labeller below the baseline): the signal's quality is a precondition, not a given. Relabelling (adding positives) is not one of the project's three arms.

## Discrepancies

- With the master document: none (not cited). With RLHN (`rlhn-2505.16967.md`): the baseline relabelling there is quoted here at 0.515 on the same table; consistent with that note's 0.515.
- Internal: the abstract says the combined strategy improves "across datasets"; Table 1 shows datasets where the combined row is below the baseline (for example FiQA-2018 for the 110M encoder, 0.434 against 0.439, p.6).
