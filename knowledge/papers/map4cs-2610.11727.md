# MAP4CS — Chen, Liu, Ou, Zhang, Li, Wang and Zheng (arXiv 2610.11727v1, 2026, submitted to a journal), "MAP4CS: A Multi-dimensional Data Pruning Framework for Efficient Code Retriever Fine-tuning"

Source file: arXiv PDF v1 (dated 8 Oct 2026; the only version), 24 pages: body pp.1-20, references pp.20-24; no appendix. The extracted text has 24 pages and no blank page. Found by the refreshed scan of 2026-10-23 (it was not in the scan of 2026-10-17). Read because it is a 2026 code-retriever paper whose intervention is **filtering the training pairs** and which compares the filtered set against random subsets and the full set, the comparison closest in form to the project's FILTER arm.

## What it established

- A pruning pipeline for (query, code) training pairs: single-pass clustering of pair embeddings (threshold 0.55), per-pair scores for AST-structure diversity within the cluster, query-code cosine similarity (sampled across high, medium and low tiers rather than maximised), kernel density and nearest-neighbour distance; hard rules first (SHA-256 deduplication, more than 1024 tokens dropped, heuristic query filter taken from Sun et al. 2022); weighted sum 1/3, 1/3, 1/6, 1/6; keep the top 5% (Sec. 3, pp.5-10).
- At an equal 5% subset size, the pruned subset beat a random 5% subset for every model and training set reported (UniXcoder, a general-purpose text encoder (GTE base) and Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B; CSN-Java and an LLM-annotated query set), e.g. average MRR 0.166 to 0.219 (UniXcoder, CSN-Java) and 0.088 to 0.128 (Qwen3-Embedding, CSN-Java) (Table 1, p.12).
- Fine-tuning on the full sets often lowered scores below the zero-shot model: Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B averaged 0.170 zero-shot, 0.045 after full CSN-Java fine-tuning and 0.128 after the pruned 5% (Table 1, p.12).
- Ablation (one model, one training set): random 5% 0.144, cluster-stratified random 5% 0.152, full pipeline 0.161 average MRR (Table 2, p.16).

## What it did not establish

- **Not a matched-budget comparison.** All runs train 6 epochs with batch size 4 (Sec. 4.1.3, p.11), so the 5% subsets get about one twentieth of the optimiser updates of the full-data runs. "Comparable to full data at 5%" therefore mixes a data effect with an update-count effect, and the full-data runs falling far below zero-shot (Table 1) suggest the training set-up itself (batch 4, in-batch negatives only, 6 epochs) damages the models; the paper reads this as noise in the data and does not test the alternative.
- **One seed** (42) for every run (p.11), no intervals; differences such as 0.152 against 0.161 in the ablation cannot be judged.
- Hyperparameters were tuned "on the validation set" (p.11) without saying which; the evaluation includes CodeSearchNet while one training set is CSN-Java (in-domain by construction).
- The embedding used for clustering and the similarity score is a pre-trained encoder ("e.g., UniXcoder", p.6), so for the UniXcoder runs the selection signal comes from the model being trained.
- Nothing at repository level, nothing about issue texts; queries are docstrings or synthetic queries.
- The component weights (1/3, 1/6) and the cluster threshold come from "empirical tuning" or "preliminary experiments" with no reported sweep (pp.7, 10).

## Method details we need

- **Pipeline** (Figure 3, p.6; Sec. 3): cluster, score within cluster, rule filter, rank by weighted score, keep the top share.
- **Semantic tiering** (Sec. 3.3.2, p.8): pairs are sampled across high, medium and low query-code similarity on purpose, to keep "hard positives". This is the opposite direction from a K-rank filter, which drops pairs whose positive ranks low under a teacher.
- **Loss** (p.11): in-batch-negative ranking loss, batch 4, maximum length 512.

## Relation to RQ1 / RQ2 / RQ3

- RQ1: none.
- RQ2: a 2026 example of a filtering intervention on a code retriever compared with an unfiltered arm and a random arm of the same size. It shows the comparison the project plans is publishable and that filtered subsets can beat random subsets; it also shows the confound the project's design avoids: without matched optimisation steps the filtered and full arms differ in update count as well as data. It is weak evidence for any specific filter (one seed, no intervals) and no evidence about weighting or curriculum. Its "keep weak-similarity pairs on purpose" choice is a counterpoint to K-rank filtering worth naming in the report's discussion, not a design input.
- RQ3: none.

## Discrepancies

- With the project's evidence: the full-data fine-tune of Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B falling to 0.045 from 0.170 zero-shot contrasts with CORE-Bench and SweRank, where fine-tuning the same family on repository-level pairs raised scores (`core-bench-2606.11864.md`, `swerank-2505.07849.md`). The set-ups differ (batch 4 here), so this is not a contradiction of those results.
- Internal: the abstract and conclusion say the pruned 5% is "comparable to, or even superior to" full data; in Table 1 that holds for every row reported, but because full data falls below zero-shot in four of six rows, "superior to full data" is mostly "less damaged than full data". The text says Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B "improves every downstream task over both random sampling and full-data fine-tuning" (p.13); on CSN-Java its pruned CoSQA score (0.066) is still below its zero-shot score (0.076), which the sentence does not claim against but which matters for the "less is more" reading.
