# RLHN — Thakur, Zhang, Ma and Lin (arXiv 2505.16967v2, EMNLP 2025 Findings), "Hard Negatives, Hard Lessons: Revisiting Training Data Quality for Robust Information Retrieval with LLMs"

Source file: arXiv PDF v2 (dated 18 Oct 2025), 20 pages (body 9 pages, limitations and references pp.9-14, appendices A-D pp.14-20: backbone choice, leave-one-out table, the judging instruction text, per-dataset curves, human-validation interface, significance plots). The extracted text has 20 pages and no blank page. Text retrieval, not code: included as the scan's example of 2025 work on training-data quality (false negatives, pruning, relabelling) for retrievers; whether it transfers to code is untested.

## What it established

- On the BGE retrieval training collection (15 datasets, 1.6M query-passage pairs), fine-tuning on a pruned seven-dataset subset (680K pairs, 42.5% of the original) gives higher BEIR nDCG@10 for a base encoder (0.529 against 0.519 for the full set); the subset was chosen by leaving one dataset out at a time (Figure 4 and Sec. 4, p.5).
- An LLM cascade (a cheap judge on all pairs, a stronger judge on the pairs it flags) finds hard negatives that are actually relevant ("false negatives"): up to 56% of MS MARCO training pairs and about 3% of SciDocs pairs have one, and MS MARCO holds 91.6% of all flagged pairs (Sec. 3.3, p.4).
- Three treatments of the flagged negatives were compared: drop the whole pair, drop only the false negatives, or relabel them as positives. Relabelling gives the best BEIR average for both the base encoder (0.515) and a 7B decoder encoder (0.518), and gains of 0.7-1.4 points on BEIR and 1.7-1.8 on a zero-shot benchmark (abstract p.1; Table 3, p.6).
- Compared against two alternatives: sampling hard negatives with a margin below the positive's score, which is lower, and distilling from a cross-encoder, which is comparable on one benchmark and higher on the other (Sec. 5, p.7).
- A reranker used as the false-negative detector finds only 52.5% to 63.8% of what the cascade finds (Table 7, p.8). A human check of 670 query-negative pairs agrees with the stronger judge better than with the cheaper one (p.8).
- Relabelled data from random subsets of 100K to 680K pairs shows monotone gains with amount of data (Figure 5, p.8).

## What it did not establish

- Nothing about code, repositories or issue texts. False negatives in the project's pool (other functions in the same repository that are also relevant to a pull-request query) are not studied.
- The pruning of datasets was chosen using BEIR scores (leave-one-out evaluated on BEIR, then the pruned set evaluated on BEIR), so the 0.529 is selected on the evaluation benchmark; seven of the 14 BEIR datasets are described as out-of-domain, which the authors use as a check (Sec. 4, p.5; Table 3, p.6).
- No seeds; significance is shown with a ranger plot over datasets (Figures 10-11, p.19) for one model per size.
- The judges are closed-source models (versions in Sec. 3.3, p.4); cost about 300 USD for the first stage and 3,000 USD for the second (pp.4-5). Reproduction with open judges is not tested.
- The cascade is not run on code pools, and the 56% for MS MARCO shows the rate varies over a factor of nearly 20 between datasets, so no rate can be assumed for another domain.

## Method details we need

- **Judge cascade** (Sec. 3.1, p.3; Figure 2, p.3): the cheap judge sees all pairs and flags those with a possible false negative (recall-oriented); the stronger judge re-judges the flagged pairs (precision-oriented). Up to 25 negatives are judged per call, temperature 0.1, chain-of-thought format; instruction text in Figure 6 (p.16).
- **Treatments** (Sec. 3.2, p.3): remove the instance; remove the false negatives; relabel them as ground truth (RLHN).
- **Training** (Sec. 4, p.5): InfoNCE with one positive, seven hard negatives and in-batch negatives (128 in all), learning rate 2e-5, up to 4-5 epochs, maximum length 350 tokens, with a base encoder (110M) and a 7B decoder as retrievers and a 3B decoder as reranker.
- **Evaluation**: BEIR (16 datasets after dropping two) and a zero-shot benchmark of five English domains; nDCG@10 (Sec. 4, p.5).
- **Baselines** (Sec. 4, p.5): hard-negative mining with a 95% margin (Moreira et al.), and cross-encoder distillation.

## Relation to RQ1 / RQ2 / RQ3

- RQ1: none.
- RQ2: closest recent evidence that supervision quality, not only quantity, changes a retriever: removing about 58% of the data, or relabelling false negatives, raised retrieval metrics on a general text benchmark at a fixed model and loss. It supports "filtering and relabelling are worth testing" and gives no matched-optimisation comparison; its pruning was selected on the evaluation set (see above). Its quality signal is an LLM judge, a different signal from K-rank; it does not use the model being trained as the judge.
- RQ3: none.
