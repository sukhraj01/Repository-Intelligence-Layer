# NPC — Zhang, Gong, He, Liu, Guo, Lv and Guo (arXiv 2311.03798v1, Findings of EMNLP 2023), "Noisy Pair Corrector for Dense Retrieval"

Source file: arXiv PDF v1 (7 Nov 2023), 13 pages (body pp.1-9, references pp.9-11, appendices A-D pp.12-13: qualitative examples, dataset sizes, a perplexity-with-hard-negatives argument, two further integrations). Includes a code-search evaluation (StaQC, SO-DS) with a code encoder as backbone.

## What it established

- Mismatched query-positive pairs ("false positives") are a distinct failure from false negatives and can be detected by the retriever's own loss: with a retriever that has had a warm-up, the loss of each training pair against randomly sampled easy negatives is fitted with a two-component Gaussian mixture each epoch, and the pair is called clean if its posterior of the low-loss component exceeds 0.5 (Sec. 3.1, pp.3-4).
- Detected pairs are not simply dropped for the contrastive term: noise-flagged pairs lose the contrastive term and every pair gets a consistency term towards a moving-average copy of the retriever (Eq. 8, p.4).
- On code search with a pretrained code encoder (UniXcoder), StaQC and SO-DS, the method beats the same encoder trained without it: StaQC MRR 30.39 against 28.04 (in-batch negatives) and 31.36 against 28.63 (hard negatives); SO-DS MRR 30.96 against 28.29 and 32.49 against 29.97 (Table 1, p.5). Single run per row; no intervals.
- On text with synthetic noise (queries randomly re-paired), plain training collapses with the noise rate (NQ R@20 with BM25 negatives: 63.36 at 20% noise, 49.70 at 50%, Table 2, p.6) while the method stays near training on the cleaned data (79.76 at 20%).
- Ablations (Table 3, p.7): at 50% noise on NQ (hard negatives) detection alone gives R@20 77.59 and correction alone 58.63, both 80.07 together, against 54.63 for neither. Iterating detection every epoch beats detecting once (R@1 50.07 against 47.29, Table 5, p.8). Computing the loss with hard negatives instead of random ones breaks detection (R@1 42.81, Table 5, p.8; argument in Appendix C, p.12).
- Warm-up length: 1 to 10 epochs similar (R@1 50.58, 50.03, 50.07), 20 epochs clearly worse (38.09), attributed to overfitting to the noise (Table 4, p.7; Sec. 4.4, p.7).

## What it did not establish

- The real-noise evidence is two code-search benchmarks without noise labels: the gain is attributed to detection by an ablation on StaQC (R@1 and R@3 columns in Table 3), and a handful of flagged pairs are shown (Table 9, p.13). Detection precision and recall against any human label are not reported.
- The detector is the retriever being trained, so no independent-teacher comparison exists; the failure when warm-up is too long is the only evidence on the cost of that.
- Query-document positives are single answers; a repository query with several correct units is not modelled (all other documents are treated as negatives).
- Single runs, no variance; hyperparameter sets differ between the datasets (Sec. 4.2, p.5).

## Method details we need

- Detection (Sec. 3.1, pp.3-4): Eq. 2 is the negative log softmax of the annotated pair against m random (not hard) negatives; a two-component mixture is fitted over all pairs each epoch by EM; clean = posterior of the lower-mean component above 0.5 (set to 0.5 in all runs).
- Correction (Sec. 3.2, p.4): a moving-average copy of the retriever supplies a soft target distribution over the candidate set; loss = clean-flag x contrastive + KL(student, teacher) (Eq. 8).
- Procedure (Algorithm 1, p.4): warm up, then each epoch detect and train. Backbone for code: a code encoder, cosine similarity, temperature 20 (Sec. 4.2, p.5).

## Relation to RQ1 / RQ2 / RQ3

- RQ1, RQ3: none.
- RQ2: closest published design to a loss-based WEIGHT or FILTER arm for code: pair confidence comes from the trained model's own loss after a warm-up (the self-teacher of Q52), re-estimated every epoch, with a soft consistency term; the reported ablations say that (a) re-estimation matters, (b) the confidence must be computed against easy negatives, not the hard ones the loss trains on, and (c) the warm-up has an interior optimum. These are three design questions for a K-rank weight that the project's pilots have not touched (static K-rank from a frozen teacher; no re-estimation; hard-negative interaction unmeasured). The evidence is single-run and noise is synthetic or unlabelled.

## Discrepancies

- With the master document: not cited; adds a self-judging loss-based detector and an EMA teacher, neither of which is among the four arms.
- Internal: the abstract and text call the detector "perplexity between the annotated and easy-negative documents"; Eq. 2 is a softmax cross-entropy over one positive and m negatives, not language-model perplexity. Naming only.
